For most restaurant operators, revenue leakage is not obvious. It doesn't appear as a single large error or a clear line item on a report. Instead, it shows up as small inconsistencies—slight overcharges, subtle discrepancies, and misaligned fees that accumulate over time. Left unchecked, these issues can quietly erode margins across every location.

This example use case illustrates how a 12-unit pizza chain leveraged FohBoh.ai's Revenue Recovery Modules—M01 (Delivery Fee Recovery) and M02 (Merchant Fee Recovery)—to identify and recover $4,200 per month per location in combined leakage.

The starting point

Like most operators, the chain relied on standard reporting from delivery service providers (DSPs), payment processors, and POS-generated summaries. All systems appeared to reconcile at a high level. There were no obvious red flags. But there was also no independent validation.

What FohBoh identified

Using FohBoh | Sentry™ and the deterministic certification engine (MGE™), the platform analyzed both delivery and merchant fee structures across all locations. The findings were consistent and measurable.

M01 — Delivery Fee Recovery

  • Commission inconsistencies across similar orders
  • Misapplied fee tiers and rate structures
  • Variances between contracted rates and actual charges

M02 — Merchant Fee Recovery

  • Processor rate creep over time
  • Misclassified transactions impacting fee percentages
  • Hidden surcharges and non-transparent fees

Individually, these issues were small. Together, they resulted in $4,200/month/location in recoverable revenue.

Why this matters

The critical difference was not just detection—it was certification. Every variance identified by FohBoh was calculated using deterministic rules, linked directly to source records, and documented in a structured, traceable format. This eliminated ambiguity.

Instead of saying "We think there's an issue," the operator could say: "Here is the certified variance, the rule applied, and the exact source record."

Operational impact

Beyond recovery, the operator gained visibility into systemic fee inconsistencies, confidence in financial data accuracy, and control over vendor relationships. More importantly, revenue recovery became repeatable. This was no longer a one-time audit—it became an ongoing governance process embedded into operations.

The bigger takeaway

Industry-wide, restaurants lose an estimated 3–7% of revenue to fee leakage across delivery and payment systems. Most of it goes unnoticed. This example demonstrates what happens when that leakage is identified, quantified, and proven.

FohBoh doesn't just surface discrepancies. It certifies them. And that distinction changes everything—because in today's environment, insight is helpful, but proof is powerful.
